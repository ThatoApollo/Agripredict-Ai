AgriPredict AI - Dataset Description

This dataset was compiled to investigate provincial maize yield prediction in Free State, Mpumalanga and North West. It contains 72 records covering 24 agricultural seasons, from 2000/01 to 2023/24, with one record per province per season.

Maize production and planted-area records came from Grain SA's Crop Estimates Committee (CEC) compilation. Relevant CEC reports were used to investigate discrepancies and update superseded figures. White and yellow commercial maize were combined, and yield was calculated by dividing total production in tonnes by planted area in hectares.

Daily rainfall and temperature data came from NASA POWER. Three sample locations were selected in each province, and their daily values were averaged equally. Weather from 1 October to 31 January was summarised into October-December rainfall, January rainfall and October-January mean temperature. These were matched to the crop records by province and season. The provisional prediction date is 15 February.

Checks covered missing values, duplicates, units, crop totals, weather dates and the final join. No missing values were filled. The prepared file contains the yield target, three weather predictors, province, season and timing information.

The data are divided chronologically, keeping all three provinces from each season together:
Training: 2000/01-2015/16 (48 records).
Validation: 2016/17-2019/20 (12 records).
Final test: 2020/21-2023/24 (12 records).

Although the dataset covers many years, 72 records are a small sample for machine learning. Provinces can also share weather conditions in the same season, so the records should not be treated as fully independent observations. The 48 training records provide a starting point for testing simple models, but they limit the complexity that can be supported. Linear regression will therefore be compared with a historical baseline, while random forest will be assessed cautiously and the neural network will remain experimental.

The dataset is suitable for a small capstone experiment, but its ability to support useful predictions has not yet been established. Model evaluation will determine whether the selected approaches improve on the baseline. The final test covers only four seasons, so its results will provide limited evidence of performance under future conditions. Selecting this dataset as the final project version does not mean that reliable forecasting has been proven.

Weather values represent sampled locations rather than the whole province, and historical data versions available at each prediction date were not verified. These limitations, together with the small sample, must be reflected in the report and dashboard. The system's outputs should be presented as experimental provincial estimates for planning scenarios rather than dependable forecasts for individual farms.

Grain SA: https://grainsa.co.za/applied-economics/crop-estimates/
